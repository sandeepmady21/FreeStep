# Current products and a practical path to standing, perching and walking

Research checked **15 October 2026**. Purpose: choose a feasible FreeStep first
prototype for adjustable adult fit and a person able to stand through one leg.
This is a broad representative review of commercial categories and relevant
research, not an exhaustive market census, clinical prescription, patent search,
or independent comparison trial. The evidence below comes from manufacturers,
primary research, clinical organizations and standards publishers. Vendor claims
are not evidence that FreeStep achieves the same outcomes.

## Findings that change the design

1. **Rising, transferring, walking and perching are different load cases.** A
   transfer platform with feet on a plate cannot provide ordinary walking until
   the feet/stride are free and body support remains effective.
2. Products combine these tasks, but with substantial support structures and
   fitting. Our earlier low-mass, narrow-base, full-lift and autonomous-driving
   claims were not supported by corresponding engineering evidence.
3. For a first prototype, one guided powered lift with passive wheel locks and
   compatible pelvic support is simpler to verify than two independent actuators
   plus a powered drivetrain. This is our engineering choice, not a clinical result.
4. Perching can also be solved by a separate stable stool. An integrated lift
   should earn its complexity by demonstrating easier entry/exit and useful reach.
5. Supported walking should follow as a distinct configuration and validation
   stage. Changing brake state under a supported person needs its own safety case.

### Research benchmark, not a current commercial substitute

[MIT E-BAR, ICRA 2025](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/b4e06e3b-683c-47e3-a61c-193ba66477be)
integrates body-weight support, postural transitions/ambulation and harness-free
fall catching in a research robot. The paper describes an optimized 18-bar
linkage, special mobile base and airbags. These are substantial mechanisms;
adding soft waist pads to a narrow frame does not reproduce them. There is no
verified retail E-BAR offer in this review. Its useful lesson is to optimize the
trajectory and floor reaction forces together. P0 explicitly does not claim fall
catching or autonomous mobility.

Rifton's older TRAM is historical: the manufacturer states it has been
[discontinued](https://www.rifton.com/education-center/articles/tram-discontinued)
and points to E-Pacer. References to TRAM are useful engineering history, not a
current new-device procurement recommendation.

## How to solve sit-to-stand

### Choose support based on ability, not an average person

The user reports that the intended person can stand on one leg. That supports
studying an active-assisted transfer, but does not establish endurance, balance,
safe ankle loading or trunk/arm control. Arjo's
[standing-sling guidance](https://www.arjo.com/en-us/products/patient-handling/slings/standing-and-walking-slings/sara-flex-standing-sling/)
describes active transfer candidates as having some upper/lower body strength and
weight-bearing ability in at least one leg. It does not authorize this prototype.

If a person cannot safely contribute to an active rise, evaluate a full-body
hoist/other assessed transfer solution instead of increasing FreeStep motor force.
For an ankle fracture, weight-bearing permission follows the treating clinician;
[AAOS guidance](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/ankle-fractures-broken-ankle)
explains that loading too early can affect alignment/healing. Do not hard-code a
recovery timeline or infer safe loading from elapsed weeks.

### Establish the path before selecting a motor

Our engineering proposal is an adjustable mock-up that maps seated access,
forward trunk motion, seat-off, hip extension, stable standing and controlled
return. Measure force and support position throughout. A fixed vertical lift can
be unsuitable if it prevents forward motion; a fixed forward incline can also
be unsuitable for another body size or chair. The path, support geometry and
actuator alignment must be solved together.

Select a single guided mast to avoid the original unsynchronized dual-lift failure.
The guide resists bending and rotation; the actuator is aligned to its rated axial
load. Use a matched OEM system with documented secondary retention, end limits,
overload protection and independent recovery. [LINAK's safety documentation](https://www.linak.co.uk/segments/medline-careline/actuator-safety-features/)
describes features such as safety nuts and manual lowering; their presence and
specifications must be confirmed on the exact ordered model. A generic self-locking
lead screw and ESP32 relay do not establish equivalent protection.

### Solve seated support application

The support must be placeable while the person remains safely seated. A closed
seat cannot be assumed to slide beneath a loaded pelvis. P0 uses an engineered
attachment system for compatible rated pelvic/thigh support, with forearm supports
and fitted perch seating. Validate pressure, sliding and release under fault load.
Avoid underarm lifting, unassessed knee pressure and a waist belt presented as
fall arrest. Commercial sling attachments must be proven compatible with the
custom structure rather than casually clipped to a handrail.

### Park, lift, retain, lower

For P0, all wheel/swivel locks are applied before rise and remain applied at the
worktop. The pendant is caregiver hold-to-run. Release or any unsafe feedback
removes requests, but hardwired protections must also stop motion without the CPU.
At perch height, an engineered positive rest lock takes the static load; its
unloading procedure and emergency lowering must remain possible after power loss.
Never equate active hub braking with power-off holding. Dedicated holding brakes
are a separate function; [Maxon describes their unpowered holding behavior](https://support.maxongroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004294873-Brake-What-to-consider).

## How to add walking

**Walking remains outside P0's released behavior.** The architecture to investigate
next is a manually propelled, continuously supported gait frame, drawing from
E-Pacer/Sara Plus. It need not have powered hub wheels. The software shipped here
requires locked brake feedback and must not be used for occupied rolling.

The proposed later sequence is:

1. Rise with the frame parked and the person continuously supported.
2. Preserve a fitted pelvic walking support and forearm support while withdrawing
   the solid perch/footplate from the full stride envelope. No unsupported interval
   is allowed while exchanging supports. Validate the mechanism under dummy load.
3. Establish a separately reviewed walking configuration: mechanical rolling
   resistance, accessible running and parking brakes, directional caster behavior,
   low-speed manual movement and an attending caregiver. Releasing a wheel lock
   must never release the vertical support.
4. Test stepping loads, foot clearance, side shifts, turns, stopping, backward
   movement and fatigue. The walking support must permit hip motion without
   driving the frame away or trapping the knees. Revalidate stability with worst
   caster orientation and a moving person, not only a parked dummy.
5. Park again before perching, lowering or changing supports. Any power failure
   must retain vertical support and permit a controlled recovery.

For a non-weight-bearing injured leg, standing on the healthy leg does not prove
safe hopping or knee-scooter ability. A physiotherapist may instead select an
appropriate walking frame/crutches or seated mobility. [South Tees NHS walking-frame guidance](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/how-to-use-a-walking-frame-or-wheeled-walking-frame/)
describes different methods for weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing use; it
does not validate free-rolling gait with this device. Do not ask a weak user to
hop a threshold. Address the route or use another assessed mobility option.

If later powered assistance is justified, add it after manual supported gait is
proven. Required work includes speed/torque feedback, independent stopping,
runaway/rollback behavior, traction loss, battery/full-charge regenerative limits,
and loss of grip. RT.2/Camino features are not implemented by merely mapping
forearm pressure to PWM. P0 removes that unsafe shortcut entirely.

### Measuring partial weight bearing correctly

Our own quasi-static force balance is:

`body weight = healthy-foot load + injured-foot load + forearm support + pelvic support`

During movement, inertial forces also matter. Measuring forearms alone cannot
solve for injured-foot load because the other terms are unknown. A total support
scale can measure aggregate offloading, not which foot carries the remainder.
For research on limb-specific loading, evaluate calibrated foot force plates or
appropriately validated in-shoe sensing against a reference across the actual
tasks. Remove any clinical feedback feature until accuracy and use are established.

The prior FSR-to-kilograms linear mapping is withdrawn. [Interlink's integration
guide](https://www.interlinkelectronics.com/downloads/integration-guides/fsr-400-series-integration-guide.pdf)
documents mechanically dependent response, hysteresis and drift. Such sensors
may serve as presence inputs after validation, not an uncalibrated clinical scale.

## How to solve kitchen perching

First compare an appropriately fitted conventional perching stool plus an assessed
transfer aid. For an integrated solution, support the pelvis and trunk, provide a
comfortable foot position and keep the work close. Adjust seat height independently
of arm position; a high narrow saddle is not suitable for every body or task.

Test the combined loaded system against overturning and sliding in every reach
direction. The actual support polygon is defined by wheel-floor contacts, including
swivel orientation. Large posture changes dominate a small low-mounted battery.
Start with cool, lightweight props and defined reach zones; progress to normal
kitchen tasks only after physical and clinical release. No P0 claim covers hot
liquids, heavy pans, wet floors or unsupported reaching.

## Tradeoffs and next decisions

The old product promise attempted narrow bathroom access, low mass, powered
body lifting, occupied driving and autonomous retrieval together. P0 prioritizes
a verifiable static load path and predictable operation. Final dimensions follow
stability and body-fit work. A door route that cannot accommodate the required
base is an environmental incompatibility, not a reason to shrink the loaded base.

Supplier quotes must cover the OEM lift system, compatible body support, guide,
structural fabrication, wheel locks, safety hardware and recovery provisions.
Add assembly, fitting, validation, maintenance and service before comparing
commercial prices. No revised low BOM or retail price is claimed. Rental is a
business hypothesis for temporary rehabilitation, to test with families/clinics.

Before manufacturing, obtain the applicable full standards and review final
classification. Public scopes identify [ISO 10535:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/72711.html)
for transfer hoists/body supports and [ISO 11199-2:2021](https://committee.iso.org/standard/76652.html?browse=ics)
for rollators. Neither a software pass count nor an OEM component certificate
certifies the assembled FreeStep. The full standards and jurisdiction-specific
requirements have not been assessed here.

## Evidence still needed

- Real adult fit data and chair/worktop/home-route measurements; the provisional
  150–185 cm/50–100 kg study envelope is not a measured coverage percentage.
- Selected OEM part numbers, manuals, supplier confirmation and actual prices.
- A single mechanically reviewed CAD revision and compatible support attachments.
- Physical load retention, brake/slide/tip, sustained perch and recovery tests.
- A clinician-reviewed study protocol before any person uses the prototype.
- For walking, separate gait/foot-clearance and moving-support validation.
